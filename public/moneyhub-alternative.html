Moneyhub is closing — here are the best alternatives for UK investors

Moneyhub has confirmed its consumer-facing app will shut down in August 2026. If you have been using it to track ISAs, pensions, or net worth, you need to find a replacement — and migrate your data — before that deadline. This is an honest comparison of the four strongest options.

May 2026 · 8 min read

Background

What was Moneyhub?

Moneyhub was one of the UK's longest-running open banking aggregation platforms, letting users connect bank accounts, pensions, investments, and property in one view. It was used both by consumers directly and by financial advisers through its B2B platform. For many UK investors, it was the only tool that offered a reasonably comprehensive picture of total wealth — ISAs, SIPPs, current accounts, and mortgages all in one place.

The closure of its consumer product reflects a broader pattern: open banking aggregation is expensive to maintain, provider connections break frequently, and the economics of consumer finance apps remain difficult. Moneyhub is concentrating on its institutional and adviser business. For its retail users, August 2026 is the cut-off.

What to do now: Export your Moneyhub data before the shutdown. Most alternatives listed here support CSV import or manual setup. Give yourself at least a month to migrate so you do not lose historical data or continuity of your net worth record.

Alternative 1

Paddock

Our pick for wealth tracking

Paddock is a privacy-first wealth tracker built specifically for UK investors. Where Moneyhub relied on open banking connections to pull in your data automatically, Paddock takes the opposite approach: you enter your own balances, typically once a month, and the app handles the maths — net worth, allocations, projections, FX conversion.

The result is an app that feels more like a deliberate financial journal than a live dashboard. That suits a particular type of user very well: someone who checks in on their wealth periodically, wants to understand long-term trajectory, and does not want the fragility that comes with bank connections breaking every few weeks.

Paddock understands UK account structures natively. ISAs, Stocks and Shares ISAs, SIPPs, cash savings, and property all have first-class support — not just generic "asset" fields. It works on both web and iOS, with a native iPhone app that has received strong reviews for its design quality. There is a free tier to start, with a subscription unlocking projections and additional account capacity.

Alternative 2

WealthR

Strong free option

WealthR is a free, web-based wealth tracker with a strong emphasis on UK tax tools and FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) planning. It is built as a progressive web app rather than a native application, which means it runs in the browser and can be added to your home screen — but it is not a true native iOS or Android app.

The tax calculator tools are a genuine differentiator. WealthR can model ISA allowance usage, capital gains estimates, and pension contribution headroom in ways that most apps — including Moneyhub — did not. For users who care about FIRE numbers, the built-in safe withdrawal rate and coast-FIRE tools are useful and clearly presented.

The trade-off is the PWA format. On mobile it is functional, but it lacks the polish and reliability of a native app. There is no iOS App Store distribution, which means no Face ID, no widget support, and the occasional quirk from browser-based storage. For users who primarily track on desktop, that matters less. For anyone who wants a great mobile experience, it is a real limitation.

Strengths
  • Completely free
  • Tax calculator tools (ISA, CGT, pension)
  • FIRE planning and projections
  • UK-aware account types
Trade-offs
  • PWA only — no native iOS app
  • Mobile experience is limited
  • Smaller product team
  • No multi-currency support
Alternative 3

WealthView

iOS only

WealthView takes a straightforward proposition: a one-time purchase price, all data stored on your device, no subscription. For users who are wary of recurring fees or cloud storage of financial data, that combination is genuinely appealing. The app is clean, quick to set up, and well-rated on the App Store.

The significant limitation is platform: WealthView is iOS-only, with no web access. If you need to check your portfolio on a laptop or a non-Apple device, there is no way in. Syncing across devices is handled through iCloud, which works well if you are fully inside the Apple ecosystem — and is a friction point if you are not.

UK tax wrapper support is partial. You can set up ISA and SIPP accounts, but the app does not model allowances, contributions, or withdrawal rules with the same depth as Paddock or WealthR. It is better suited to straightforward net worth tracking than to active tax planning. For users who want a clean, private ledger and never need web access, it is a reasonable choice.

Strengths
  • One-time price, no subscription
  • Fully on-device storage
  • Clean, fast iOS app
  • No account required
Trade-offs
  • iOS only — no web access
  • Limited UK wrapper depth
  • iCloud sync only
  • No projections or planning tools
Alternative 4

Emma

Best for bank linking and budgeting

Emma is the most Moneyhub-like option on this list in one important sense: it links to your bank accounts automatically via open banking. If the reason you used Moneyhub was the automatic sync — waking up to an updated view of everything without manual input — Emma is the closest like-for-like replacement on that dimension.

It is primarily a budgeting and spending tool, though. Emma excels at helping you see where your money goes, identify subscriptions, and track spending categories. Its wealth tracking features are less developed — the focus is on transactions, not long-term trajectory or tax wrappers. For Moneyhub users who primarily cared about total wealth across ISAs and pensions, Emma will feel like a step down in that department.

A practical note: Emma's version 3 rollout received a significant volume of user complaints around feature removals and changed pricing. Some features from earlier versions were moved behind paywalls. It is worth reading recent App Store reviews before committing. The free tier is useful for basic use, and the Pro plan unlocks most features — but the history of pricing changes is worth knowing about. Emma also uses your financial data to improve and personalise the product, which is standard for open banking apps but worth being aware of if privacy is a priority.

Strengths

  • Strong budgeting and spending tools
  • Free tier available
  • iOS and Android
  • Large, active team
Trade-offs

  • v3 complaints around feature changes
